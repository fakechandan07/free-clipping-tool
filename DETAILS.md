# Five Star Clipper: the details

Paste a **Twitch, YouTube or Kick** link, live or recorded. When chat goes crazy, or
the streamer loses it, it clips the moment and turns it into a ready-to-post
vertical short: **the streamer's face on top (30%)**,
**gameplay below (70%)**, and captions in a bold GTA-style font. For **IRL**
streams, the stream fills **the middle 80% of the phone** (blurred edges above and below).

Free, runs on your own computer (**Mac or Windows**), and keeps going as long as
it's on.

*Installing: see the main [README](../README.md).*

## How to use it

1. **Double-click "Five Star Clipper"** (on your Desktop, or the Mac / Windows icon in the main folder). The clipper page opens
   in your browser. The clipper itself runs quietly in the background, with no
   window to keep open. (On a Mac you can also run `./clip` in Terminal.)
2. **Paste a Twitch, YouTube or Kick link** (live or recorded) and hit **GO**.
   You can watch up to 6 live streamers at once, and scan recorded videos.
3. That's it. When chat goes crazy, a clip is saved, **CLIP SECURED** pops
   up, and a few seconds later the vertical short appears under **Your Clips**,
   ready to download.

- **Close the browser tab anytime.** Clipping keeps going in the background.
  Double-click the Desktop icon again to reopen the page.
- **To stop clipping completely:** click **⏻ Stop clipper** at the top of the
  page. Restarting your computer also stops it.
- If a streamer is offline, it waits and starts by itself when they go live.
- The streamers you add are remembered, so next time you open it, they're
  already being watched.
- Your clips are saved in the `tool/clips/` folder. Background messages go
  to `clipper.log`, in case something goes wrong.
- **If you move or rename this folder,** the ⭐ icon inside it keeps working.
  Only the Desktop shortcut breaks: delete it and double-click the icon in the folder again.

## What links work

| Paste this | What happens |
|---|---|
| Twitch channel (`twitch.tv/name` or just `name`) | **Watches live** and clips as it happens. Waits if they're offline. |
| YouTube channel or live (`youtube.com/@name`, `/@name/live`, a live video link) | **Watches live**, with YouTube live chat. Waits if the stream is scheduled. |
| Twitch past broadcast (`twitch.tv/videos/123...`) | **Scans** the whole VOD with its chat replay, then clips the best moments. |
| YouTube past stream | **Scans** with its chat replay (if the stream had one). |
| Any YouTube video | **Scans** using the streamer's reactions (laughing, screaming) and timestamps in the comments. |

**Scanning a recorded video** (pick **Top 5 / 10 / 20** next to the GO button):

1. **Chat replay:** it reads the chat replay. Very busy chats are sampled
   evenly, so even a 13-hour stream takes a minute or two.
2. **Audio:** it streams the audio through the laugh/scream detector without
   saving it, much faster than real time.
3. **Picks** the best moments with the same 🔥 score as live clips.
4. **Downloads only those parts** in 1080p (not the whole 40 GB VOD), then
   smart-trims them and makes the shorts.

The **Scanning** section on the page shows progress, time left, and a Cancel
button. Clips from videos are tagged **📼 at 1:23:45** so you know where in the
video they came from.

**Good to know:**
- YouTube sometimes asks to "confirm you're not a bot". If a YouTube link
  fails with that, wait a bit, or update with
  `.venv/bin/pip install -U yt-dlp yt-dlp-ejs`.
- Twitch deletes past broadcasts after 7-60 days, and some are
  subscriber-only.
- YouTube live chat arrives 10-20 seconds late (that's YouTube); clips still
  line up because the video is delayed about the same.
- Credit the streamer: every short already shows `twitch.tv/name` or
  `youtube.com/@name`. Downloading other people's YouTube videos is against
  YouTube's rules, so take clips down if a creator asks.

## Ready to post

**See more** under every short and long video shows upload-ready text, each
with a **Copy** button:

- **Title:** for example "IShowSpeed tried to outrun the cops 💀 | GTA 6"
- **Hook:** the big text shown in the first 2 seconds of the short
- **Description:** what was said, credit to the streamer, and hashtags
- **Hashtags:** #shorts #streamer #gta6 #funnymoments …
- **Keywords / tags:** for YouTube's tags box

Use **Mark as posted** once you've uploaded a clip, and the **📤 To post**
filter shows only the clips you haven't posted yet, best first.

**Shorts are edited for you:** big hook text with a whoosh at the start, a
punch-in zoom (plus a camera shake when the streamer screams) and a deep "boom"
on the reaction, and emoji above caption keywords (😂 💀 🚔 💰 😱). The sound
effects are generated by the tool itself, so there's no copyright risk. Use
`--no-effects` for plain shorts.

**Two POVs:** when two streamers you're watching clip the same moment (playing
together), you also get a split-screen "duo" short with both reactions.

**No duplicates:** a moment that's already clipped is skipped.

## Settings (bottom of the Shorts tab)

- **Start with my computer:** opens by itself when you log in (on a Mac it also restarts itself if it crashes).
- **Auto-cleanup:** under 3 GB free, the oldest clips go to the Trash (posted
  ones first). ⭐ Starred clips are never touched.
- **Computer notification** (Mac or Windows) when a clip scores at least 80 🔥 (you can change the
  number).
- **Phone alerts** (free): install the **ntfy** app and subscribe to the name
  shown in Settings.

## Kick

Kick live streams and past broadcasts work (recording, scans, shorts, long
videos). **Kick chat isn't reachable right now** (Kick changed its chat
server), so Kick streams are clipped from the streamer's reactions and
loudness instead of chat.

## Long videos (10-15 minute YouTube videos)

Open the **🎬 Long videos** tab at the top of the page.

1. **Paste recorded streams or videos**, one per line, up to 6. Any mix of
   Twitch past broadcasts and YouTube videos works, for example several
   streamers' first GTA 6 streams.
2. **Type what the video is about** ("Streamers play GTA 6 for the first time")
   and pick **10, 12 or 15 minutes**.
3. **Press BUILD STORYBOARD.** It scans every stream (chat, laughs, screams,
   who the streamer is) and lays out the video:
   - **Cold open:** a 6-second teaser of the climax
   - **Title card** with your topic and the streamers' names, read out by the narrator
   - **First impressions:** each streamer's first minutes
   - **Build-up:** good moments in story order, switching between streamers
     and mixing kinds (cops, funny, money, hype…) so it never repeats itself.
     Story moments (missions, heists, cops) get more time than quick funny ones.
     Each chapter gets a title made from what the streamer said, like
     "Speed vs the cops", "Kai can't stop laughing" or "Chat goes crazy".
   - **The climax:** the single biggest moment, saved for near the end
   - **The ending:** how one of the streams finished
   - **Finale: "Everyone's reaction":** all the streamers' biggest
     reactions at once, in a split screen
   - **End screen (20 s):** two "watch next" boxes and a subscribe circle, laid
     out exactly where YouTube's end-screen elements go
4. **Check the storyboard.** You can rename any chapter, change or delete
   what the 🎙️ **narrator** says, untick moments you don't want, tick extra
   moments to add them, or move chapters up and down. Switches at the top turn
   each extra on or off: narrator voice, captions, jump cuts, facecam zoom,
   sound effects, background music. The page shows the video length as you go.
5. **Press 🎬 Render video.** It downloads only the chosen moments in 1080p,
   trims each one at natural pauses, and builds the video. This takes about
   15-25 minutes.

**What makes it look edited (all automatic):**
- 🎙️ **Narrator:** a built-in voice introduces the video and every chapter
  ("8 minutes in, Speed finds the cops…"). On a Mac it uses Evan, Allison or
  Joelle (for a better voice: System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content
  → System voice → Manage voices, pick an English "Enhanced" or "Premium"
  voice). On Windows it uses Mark, David or Zira.
- 🎬 **Chapter cards** before every moment (blurred frame + title).
- ✂️ **Jump cuts** remove dead air: only where nobody talks *and* it's quiet,
  and never near the big reaction.
- 🔍 **Facecam zoom:** on the big reaction it cuts to the streamer's webcam,
  full screen, for 2 seconds.
- 💬 **Captions** at the bottom, 💥 a hit sound on the big moment, 60 fps, high bitrate.
- 🖼️ **Thumbnail** from the frame where the streamer looks most shocked or
  happiest, with their face enlarged in a circle and big text.

**You get:** the video, a **thumbnail**, **5 title ideas**, and a
**description with YouTube chapter timestamps** and credits for every
streamer, ready to paste into YouTube. Everything is saved in
`clips/_long/<date>/`.

**Good to know:**
- **Background music:** put your own songs in the `tool/music/` folder
  (mp3, m4a, wav…). The video plays one quietly underneath and turns it down
  whenever someone talks. Only use music you have the rights to (for example
  the YouTube Audio Library), or the video can get copyright-claimed. With an
  empty folder there's no music.
- Chapter titles come from keywords in what the streamer said (cops, heist,
  driving, money, guns, missions, laughing...). They're usually good, but they
  don't truly understand the story, which is why you can edit them.
- It needs about 3 GB of free disk space while rendering (an 8-minute video
  is about 700 MB).

## The page

- **Wanted stars:** each streamer's card has 5 stars that fill up as chat
  heats up. All 5 flashing means chat is going crazy and it's clipping.
- **How easily it clips:** slide toward "more" for more clips, or toward
  "fewer" for only the craziest moments. It saves automatically.
- **✂ Clip now:** grab the last ~30 seconds yourself.
- **Auto / Gaming / IRL:** how that streamer's shorts are made (see below).
- **✕** stops watching a streamer. Clips you already have are kept.
- **Your Clips:** watch each short, **⬇ Download** it, ★ star the good ones,
  or open the original horizontal clip. Delete removes the clip and its short.

## How it picks moments

It doesn't just count chat messages. Every moment gets a **🔥 score (0-100)**
from:

- **Chat explosion:** how much faster than normal chat got, weighted by hype
  emotes (KEKW, OMEGALUL, Pog, 💀...) and "CLIP IT" messages.
- **Crowd:** how many *different* people reacted, so one spammer can't fake it.
- **The streamer's voice:** when the streamer suddenly gets much louder than
  normal (yelling, laughing) and chat reacts too, that counts, even in smaller
  chats. A loud game with a quiet chat doesn't.
- **Viewer clips (optional, see below):** viewers making their own Twitch
  clips of the moment.

It also **ignores fake hype**: raids and gift-sub bombs flood chat without
anything happening on stream, so chat is ignored for a bit after them. Bots
and `!commands` never count. Chat has to stay hot for a second or two, so a
random burst isn't enough.

**Back-to-back spikes become one clip:** if chat explodes again within 20
seconds, you get one longer clip with the whole story instead of two halves.

The page sorts clips **🔥 Best first** (switch to *Newest* anytime), so you
can post the top ones.

## Streamer reactions (no chat needed)

It also **watches and listens to the stream itself**, so funny and crazy
moments get caught even when chat is slow:

- **😂 Laughing, 😱 screaming / yelling, gasps, 🎉 cheering:** a sound
  recognizer (Google's YAMNet, which knows 521 kinds of sounds) listens to the
  stream twice a second.
- **😄 Big smile / 😮 shocked face:** it reads the streamer's expression on the
  facecam every couple of seconds (FER+ emotion model).
- **🗣 Hype words:** phrases like "NO WAY", "bro what", "I'm crying", "did you see
  that" in the transcript raise the clip's score.

These add up to a **streamer reaction** level (the teal line on each card's
graph). A strong reaction clips by itself, like laughing that lasts a couple
of seconds, or a scream with a shocked face. A single short chuckle doesn't.
When chat *also* goes crazy, the score goes even higher.

- Turn it on or off per streamer with the **😂 Reactions** button on their card.
- **Won't catch:** deadpan humor with no reaction.
- **Can be fooled by:** a streamer laughing at a chat message or donation.
  The 🔥 score helps you skip those.
- All of it runs on your Mac, free. On an M-series Mac it uses a small part of
  one CPU core per streamer.

## No missing context

For each moment it saves about **a minute before chat exploded**, then reads
the transcript and trims it at natural points:

- **Start:** at a pause in speech before the action, so the setup is
  included ("yo watch this..."), never mid-sentence. It aims for about 20
  seconds before chat reacted, but moves to the nearest clean pause.
- **End:** once the reaction is over and chat calms down, letting the streamer
  finish their sentence.
- Clips are usually **20-60 seconds** (up to 75 for long merged moments).

## Viewer clips (optional, free)

Open **Viewer clips** at the bottom of the page and follow the 3 steps to get
free Twitch developer keys (about 2 minutes). Then:

- Clips you have that viewers also clipped get a higher score and a
  **👀 viewer clips** tag.
- If 2+ viewers clip something chat didn't go crazy for, it gets clipped too.

The keys stay on this computer (`clips/settings.json`).

## Gaming vs IRL

Each streamer card has a switch:

| Mode | Short looks like |
|---|---|
| **Auto** (default) | Picks by itself from the stream's Twitch category. IRL, Just Chatting, Travel & Outdoors, Talk Shows, Food & Drink, Music, etc. → **IRL**. Anything else (GTA 6...) → **Gaming**. It re-checks every 5 minutes, so a streamer going from Just Chatting into GTA switches by itself. It also goes IRL when the camera fills the whole stream. |
| **Gaming** | Face on top **30%**, gameplay below **70%** |
| **IRL** | The stream fills **the middle 80%** of the phone, centered on the streamer, with a blurred copy in the thin bars above and below |

The card shows the current category and which style is being used.

## How the vertical short is made

1. It looks through the clip for the **streamer's face**. The face that stays
   in the same spot is the webcam, since game characters move around. Then
   it finds the edges of the facecam box and crops the streamer into the top
   30%, staying inside the box. Each streamer's cam position is remembered,
   in case a clip's face is hard to see.
2. The **gameplay** fills the bottom 70%, cropped from the middle of the
   screen and away from the facecam. (IRL: the stream fills the middle 80%.)
3. **Captions** are made on your Mac with whisper.cpp (free, offline): big
   outlined words, with the spoken word in yellow. Swears are masked (`SH*T`).
4. `twitch.tv/<streamer>` credit goes just under the facecam.

**It knows who the streamer is (face recognition).** Streams are full of faces
that aren't the streamer: Fortnite skins, lobby art, teammates' webcams, guests.
The streamer is the person who shows up the most. So it groups faces by *who
they are* (OpenCV's SFace model) and only ever crops **the streamer's own face**:

- **Scans:** it looks at ~30 frames across the whole video first.
- **Live:** it learns from the camera pictures it reads every 2 seconds.
- **Remembered** per streamer in `clips/<name>/.streamer.json`, so next time it
  already knows them.

If the streamer isn't on camera in a clip, the short uses full-screen gameplay
instead of someone else's face.

Files land in `clips/<streamer>/` (horizontal originals) and
`clips/<streamer>/shorts/` (vertical shorts).

## Good to know

- **First 90 seconds per streamer:** it learns how fast that chat normally
  is, so it won't auto-clip yet. ✂ Clip now works right away.
- **Slow chats** rarely produce clips. It needs several different people
  reacting at once. Slide toward "more" for smaller streamers.
- **Disk space:** each moment is about 85 MB (clip + short). The page shows
  free space, and clipping stops below 1 GB free.
- **Keeps your computer awake** while running. A closed laptop lid still sleeps
  unless it's plugged into power and a monitor.
- Chat bots (Nightbot, etc.) and `!commands` are ignored.
- **If Twitch changes something and it breaks:** run
  `.venv/bin/pip install -U streamlink`.

## Advanced: Terminal options

```bash
./clip                      # same as double-clicking the Mac icon (run inside the tool folder)
```

```bash
./clip xqc jynxzi --sensitivity 2.5
```

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| `--sensitivity 3` | Default clip level (chat this many times busier than normal) |
| `--pre 22` | Aim to start this many seconds before chat exploded (moved to the nearest pause) |
| `--context 55` | How far back it looks for a good starting point |
| `--max-len 75` | Longest clip in seconds |
| `--mode irl` | Start streamers given on the command line in IRL mode (`auto`, `gaming`, `irl`) |
| `--layout blur` | Gameplay in the middle with blurred background, instead of facecam on top |
| `--no-shorts` | Only save horizontal clips |
| `--no-effects` | Plain shorts: no hook text, zoom, shake or sound effects |
| `--no-reactions` | Don't listen/watch for the streamer laughing, screaming or looking shocked |
| `--no-captions` / `--no-censor` | Shorts without captions / without masking swears |
| `--test-clip` | Force one clip ~40s after recording starts, to check everything works |
| `--fresh` | Don't resume last session's streamers |
| `--top 10` | Recorded videos given on the command line: how many clips to make |
| `--port 9000` | Use a different port for the page |

Make shorts from existing clips:

```bash
.venv/bin/python shorts.py clips/tfue/*.mp4
```

Run the detector tests: `.venv/bin/python test_hype.py`

## What's inside

| File | Job |
|---|---|
| `clipper.py` | Records streams into a short rolling buffer, reads chat, saves clips |
| `hype.py` | Decides when something worth clipping happened, and scores it |
| `smartcut.py` | Picks clean start/end points from the transcript |
| `twitch_api.py` | Optional viewer-clips lookup (official Twitch API) |
| `sources.py` | Understands pasted links; reads YouTube live chat and chat replays |
| `vodscan.py` | Scan mode for recorded videos and VODs |
| `metadata.py` | Titles, hooks, descriptions, hashtags and keywords |
| `housekeeping.py` | Settings, no duplicates, auto-cleanup, alerts, start with computer |
| `platformkit.py` | Everything that differs between Mac and Windows (voices, encoder, notifications, Trash) |
| `setup.sh`, `windows/` | One-time setup for Mac and for Windows |
| `longform.py` | Long videos: storyboard, title cards, chapters, finale, rendering |
| `longedit.py` | Long-video editing: narrator, jump cuts, facecam zoom, captions, music, end screen, thumbnail |
| `shorts.py` | Builds the vertical short and captions |
| `facecam.py` | Finds the streamer's face/webcam (OpenCV YuNet) |
| `identity.py` | Recognizes which face is the streamer (OpenCV SFace) |
| `reactions.py` | Hears laughing/screaming (YAMNet) and reads the streamer's expression (FER+) |
| `dashboard.py`, `web/` | The page you use. It only works on your own computer |
| `models/` | Speech (whisper base.en), face finder (YuNet), sounds (YAMNet), expressions (FER+) |
| `web/fonts/` | Passion One display font (SIL Open Font License, `OFL.txt`) |

The look is GTA-*inspired* (sunset, palms, wanted stars, outlined text), with
no Rockstar logos or artwork, so it's safe to show on your channel.

## Please clip responsibly

This tool only makes clips; what you post is up to you. Credit the streamers
(every clip's description already does), follow each platform's rules, and
respect streamers who don't want their content clipped.
